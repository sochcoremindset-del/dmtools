Understanding Board Feet: The Ultimate Guide for Woodworkers

If you’ve ever walked into a hardwood lumber yard in the US, you quickly realize they don't sell wood quite like the big box home centers do. Instead of pricing a piece of wood by the linear foot, rough hardwood is sold by volume. This specific measurement of volume is called a board foot (often abbreviated as BDFT or BF).

What Exactly is a Board Foot?

A board foot is a specialized unit of measurement for lumber volume in North America. Simply put, one board foot is equal to a piece of wood that is exactly 12 inches long, 12 inches wide, and 1 inch thick.

The Board Foot Formula

You don't need a math degree to figure out your lumber needs. The standard industry formula is incredibly straightforward:

Board Feet = (Thickness in inches × Width in inches × Length in feet) ÷ 12

Note: If you prefer to measure your length in inches instead of feet, you simply divide the total by 144 instead of 12. Both equations will give you the exact same result.

Nominal vs. Actual Dimensions

The biggest mistake beginners make when calculating board feet is using the actual surfaced dimensions of the wood rather than the nominal dimensions. Lumber mills price wood based on the rough size of the board before it goes through the planer or jointer.
